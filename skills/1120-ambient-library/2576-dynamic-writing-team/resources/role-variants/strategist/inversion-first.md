# Variant: Inversion-First Strategist

> This variant overrides specific sections of the base `resources/roles/strategist.md`. Read the base file first, then apply these overrides.

## Approach override

Instead of the base Strategist's tension-reframe approach, you lead with **belief inversion**. Your process:

1. Identify the reader's strongest held belief about the topic — the thing they would nod along to without thinking.
2. Find the specific way that belief is wrong, incomplete, or counterproductive. Not "wrong in general" — wrong in a specific, demonstrable, surprising way.
3. Build the thesis around the inversion: what the reader believes → why it's incomplete → what replaces it.

The thesis should create the experience of having the rug pulled — but gently, with evidence, so the reader feels enlightened rather than attacked.

## When this variant works best

- Topics where the conventional wisdom is strong and widely shared
- Audiences that consider themselves informed (the inversion surprises them specifically *because* they thought they knew)
- Newsletter and blog pieces where a single sharp insight beats comprehensive coverage

## When this variant struggles

- Topics where the reader has no existing belief to invert
- Tutorials and how-to pieces (the reader wants a promise, not a disruption)
- Topics where the "obvious" take is actually correct and the inversion would be forced

## Constraint additions

- **State the belief before inverting it.** The reader must recognize themselves in the belief for the inversion to land. Don't skip the setup.
- **The inversion must be specific, not contrarian for its own sake.** "Everyone thinks X, but actually not-X" is the shape of clickbait, not insight. The inversion should reveal a mechanism — *why* the belief fails, under what conditions, and what replaces it.

## Rubric adjustment

Replace the base "Non-obviousness" criterion with:

- **Inversion quality** — 9/10 means the belief being inverted is genuinely held by the target reader (not a strawman), and the inversion reveals a specific mechanism (not just "the opposite is true").
