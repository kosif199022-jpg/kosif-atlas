# Project-specific typography

Two families maximum, not two mandatory families. One strong family may be enough. Never substitute a repeated favorite pairing for studying the brand. Select for voice, audience, reading task, density, numerals, language coverage, and deployment/license constraints. At most three sizes and weights per meaningful component; hierarchy also comes from space, color, position and line length.

Before choosing, compare candidates on the actual headline, a paragraph, an action, and representative product numbers. Check mobile wraps, italics, punctuation, tabular numerals and font loading. Record why the chosen family fits this project. Do not automatically add a serif to make a page feel “premium,” or a monospace to make it look technical.

## User-supplied candidates, not defaults

Adam's screenshots (2026-09-30) name **Geist, Onest, BDO Grotesk, and Manrope**. These are sans-serif candidates, despite the message describing them as serif options. Treat all four as possibilities, not a required shortlist or implicit license grant. Inspect official foundry/distributor specimens and verify web embedding and redistribution terms before adoption. Do not assume a social screenshot supplies font files or rights.

Other families remain eligible. Preserve a client's licensed face when that is the intake choice; choose a new face when the project direction calls for it. No fixed Inter/Baskerville pairing. In the Harvest concept, Besley relates to the observed current brand while Onest supplies the newly designed operational UI; that is a project choice, not a recipe for subsequent sites.
