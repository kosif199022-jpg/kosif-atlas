# Portfolio, studio, and high-concept launch sites

Use when memorability and point of view are primary. Experimental does not mean disorienting.

## Governing-metaphor pattern

- Blink turns exchange fairness and physical network latency into the visual world: equalized points, routes, measurements, and motion.
- Content Architecture uses repository, terminal, ASCII, and committed-system language across copy, navigation, and graphics.
- Edoardo Lunardi’s portfolio treats the browser as a desktop/workbench: dense file navigation, project panes, utilities, and live experiments.
- Prism uses refraction and molecular motion as a restrained scientific motif rather than a generic tech effect.

## Rules

- Choose one metaphor that can govern typography, layout, motion, copy, and interaction.
- Keep a stable anchor: persistent navigation, project index, scroll indicator, or clearly visible action.
- Make the first interaction reveal the concept within seconds; do not require a tutorial to find the work.
- Use system-like density only when the content benefits from comparison and browsing.
- Experimental visuals should degrade gracefully and never conceal essential copy or links.
- Portfolio case studies still need: role, client/context, year, challenge, contribution, result, and artifacts.
- A single-page launch may be short. It still needs proposition, differentiation, mechanism, and action.

## Originality test

Remove the logo. If the remaining composition is immediately identifiable as a reference brand, the work is too derivative. Change the governing metaphor, proportions, content model, or interaction logic—not just colors.
