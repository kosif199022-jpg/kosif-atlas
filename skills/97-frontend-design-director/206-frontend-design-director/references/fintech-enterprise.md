# Fintech and enterprise

Use when the visitor weighs trust, switching cost, governance, and economic value.

## Two effective modes

### Operational clarity

Ramp uses plain language, bright high-contrast actions, quantified savings, dense solution breadth, testimonials, and compliance detail. Use this when the buyer is accountable for measurable business outcomes.

### Aspirational trust

Mercury pairs a regulated product with cinematic brand imagery, calm typography, friendly workflows, founder proof, and explicit disclosures. Use this when the service should feel like an upgrade in identity as well as utility.

## Recommended sequence

1. Outcome + who it is for + primary eligibility/action.
2. Early legitimacy: customer scale, regulatory context, or trusted users.
3. Product suite organized by financial job.
4. Quantified outcome and realistic workflow demonstration.
5. Controls, permissions, security, compliance, and support.
6. Switching or onboarding path.
7. Pricing, eligibility, and disclosures.
8. Repeated action with no surprise requirements.

## Rules

- Never hide material qualification, pricing conditions, or regulatory context behind atmosphere.
- Put trust proof beside the risky claim: security near account access; controls near delegation; uptime near payments.
- Replace vague “save time and money” language with a mechanism and a credible unit.
- Use customer quotes with role and operational specificity.
- Treat disclosures as designed content: readable type, clear hierarchy, and proximity to the relevant promise.
- Enterprise pages must explain deployment, governance, procurement, and support—not merely add more logos.
- Maintain visual calm. Trust is reduced by frantic animation, tiny gray text, or speculative charts.
