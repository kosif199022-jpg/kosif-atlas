# Ecommerce and physical products

Use when desire and purchase confidence matter more than explaining a software workflow. This mode is a derived extension of the corpus rather than a direct copy of one reference site.

## Recommended sequence

1. Product image or use moment with product name, price, primary variant, and purchase action.
2. One-sentence differentiator grounded in a material or outcome.
3. Detail story: form, material, construction, taste, fit, or technology.
4. Use contexts and scale cues.
5. Reviews, press, testing, or maker proof.
6. Variants, compatibility, care, shipping, returns, and warranty.
7. Cross-sell only after the primary product is understood.

## Visual modes

- **Editorial luxury:** generous whitespace, artful crops, sparse copy, restrained interface.
- **Technical product:** exploded views, labeled details, specifications, comparison.
- **Everyday utility:** use scenes, benefits, reviews, fast scannability.
- **Collection-led retail:** strong taxonomy, filters, consistent crops, clear availability.

## Rules

- Photography is the interface. Define shot roles: hero, scale, detail, in-use, variant, packaging.
- Keep price, variant, availability, shipping, and returns easy to find.
- Show real differences between variants; never rely on color names alone.
- Sticky purchase controls may help on long product pages but must not cover content or replace the primary buy block.
- Avoid software-style feature-card grids. Use editorial sequences, comparisons, or annotated product views.
- Reserve immersive 3D or scroll effects for products whose form materially benefits from them; always provide a fast static path.
