# Inner-page archetypes

Use this after choosing the site family.

## Product / capability

Hero: exact job + primary state. Then mechanism, workflow, edge cases, integrations, proof, FAQ, CTA. Keep the page narrower than the homepage; one capability is the protagonist.

## Solutions / audience

Start with the audience’s operating problem, not a renamed feature list. Show their workflow, outcomes, relevant capabilities, controls, and a customer from that segment.

## Pricing

Explain the unit before the tiers. State who each tier serves, what changes at limits, and what requires sales. Add a comparison table only after clear tier summaries. Surface overages, annual discounts, trials, refunds, and enterprise requirements.

## Customers / case studies

Index by recognizable problem or segment. Case study sequence: context → constraint → decision → implementation → quantified outcome → products used. Use quotes as evidence, not decoration.

## Enterprise

Cover deployment model, security, governance, administration, procurement, support, migration, and proof at scale. Give technical and economic buyers separate paths through the same page.

## About

Mission, origin, operating principles, people, milestones, and current direction. Avoid the generic “we’re a passionate team” page.

## Research / blog index

Use one lead story and a legible archive. Provide topic, format, date, author, and reading signal. Filters should reflect a meaningful taxonomy.

## Careers

Connect mission to actual work. Show principles through concrete practices, team context, process, location model, benefits, and open roles. Make empty states useful.

## Docs / resources

Optimize for retrieval: strong search, task-based navigation, progressive examples, stable anchors, copyable code, version context, and next steps.
